# Legal and Content Policy

Last updated: 2026-08-09

Civil AI is an independent, open-source learning tool. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or operated by any civil-service examination authority, question-setting body, government agency, or training institution. It does not provide an official score, admission prediction, policy interpretation, or guarantee of examination results.

## Bundled content

The repository may include small, maintainer-authored examples solely to demonstrate schemas, rendering, tests, and learning workflows. Such examples must not be described as official questions or copied from a commercial question bank. The project does not bundle, sell, license, or promise access to a collection of third-party examination questions.

Contributors must not submit:

- leaked, unreleased, confidential, or unlawfully obtained examination material;
- substantial copied portions of books, paid courses, question banks, explanations, websites, or databases without permission;
- content that removes attribution, provenance, access controls, or AI-generation labels;
- personal information, secrets, credentials, or material whose disclosure may harm another person or organization.

If a contribution is based on third-party material, the contributor must document its source and the permission or other lawful basis that allows redistribution under the repository license. A publicly accessible webpage is not, by itself, permission to copy and redistribute its contents.

## User imports and web research

Import and research features are user-directed tools. Search results are references for review, not a grant of copyright or proof that material is an official examination question. Retrieved material must remain a draft until the user verifies source, scope, accuracy, and permission. The software must not bypass authentication, payment, robots restrictions, technical protection measures, or a site's access rules.

Users are responsible for ensuring that their collection, use, storage, adaptation, and sharing of imported material are lawful. Maintainers should not accept requests to obtain leaked questions, reproduce a paid question bank, or publish copyrighted collections.

## AI output and public information

AI output can be inaccurate or outdated. Generated questions, explanations, summaries, scores, and study suggestions must remain distinguishable from official or human-authored materials. Current policy, syllabus, recruitment, and current-affairs claims should cite an authoritative source and its date. The project must not create false government attribution or present generated content as an official notice.

Deployers offering generative-AI functions to the public in mainland China must independently assess applicable service, content-safety, filing, security-assessment, complaint-handling, and generated-content-labeling obligations. Source-code publication alone does not establish compliance for a later public service.

## Takedown and reports

Rights holders may report a suspected infringement privately using the contact method in [`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md). A report should identify the work, repository location, rights holder, and requested action. Maintainers may restrict or remove disputed content while reviewing the report.

## No legal advice

This policy is a conservative project rule, not legal advice and not a substitute for advice from qualified counsel. Laws and deployment obligations vary by jurisdiction and by the actual product behavior.
